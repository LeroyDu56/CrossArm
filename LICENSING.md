## Free, no licence needed

- Reading the code, building it and running it to see what it does.
- Evaluating CrossArm on your own backups, including inside a company.
- Development, testing and benchmarking.
- Teaching, training, student work and personal projects.
- Working on CrossArm itself, or preparing a contribution to it.

## Needs a commercial licence

- Converting programs that are loaded on a robot doing real work.
- Converting programs delivered to a customer or to another company, whether
  or not the conversion itself is billed.
- Using CrossArm as part of a billed service, such as a cell migration or a
  robot changeover.
- Offering CrossArm, or a service built on it, to other people.

## The short version

Using CrossArm **inside** your company, without redistributing it, is not by
itself enough to stay in the free grant. What matters is whether the converted
programs are **put to work or handed over**. Trying CrossArm on a backup to see
how far it gets is free. Shipping what it produces is not.

## The evaluation mark and licence files

Every feature works without a licence. What differs is what the output says:

- **Without a licence**, every program CrossArm writes starts with the remarks
  `CrossArm EVALUATION copy` and `production use needs a licence`, and the
  conversion report says the same at the top.
- **With a licence**, those lines name the licence instead
  (`crossarm licence RC-2026-0001` and the licensee).

A licence is a small file, `crossarm.licence`, signed by the Licensor and
delivered with a commercial licence. Put it next to `CrossArm.exe`, or install
it from the window (click **Evaluation copy** at the top right). CrossArm checks
it on the computer it runs on; nothing is sent anywhere.

The mark is information, not the licence. **Removing it — by editing the
programs, the code or the executable — grants no right**: production use needs
a commercial licence whether the mark is there or not, and programs delivered
without it after such an edit are still unlicensed.
